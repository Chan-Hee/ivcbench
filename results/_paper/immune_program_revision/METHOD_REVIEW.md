## Initial judgment

The proposed reconstruction is defensible as an explicitly descriptive reanalysis. The original four questions can remain. Correcting asymmetric scoring, undefined-to-zero substitution, selection-derived null shading, and the interpretation of Pearson correlation as response magnitude does not require replacing those questions with a measurement-diagnostics figure. The answer to each original question must follow the new results, including negative or unidentifiable results.

## Program readout and comparison population

1. Use the original program lists without tuning genes, signs, weights, or cutoffs to the current results. The proposed equal-weight mean expression delta is a linear population statistic: mean over a fixed set of measured program genes of `(predicted mean - control mean)`, paired with the identical operation on `(observed mean - control mean)`. It is in the stored normalized-expression coordinates; it is not AUCell, raw-count pseudobulk fold change, validated pathway activation, or within-cell distributional recovery. Equal weights do not prevent higher-amplitude genes from contributing more to the resulting signal.
2. Preserve symmetric top-5% rank scoring of both population means as the original-readout sensitivity. Never pair a score of predicted means with cached means of cellwise nonlinear rank scores. Identity controls must return zero error and correlation one whenever the observed target is estimable.
3. Freeze one gene membership set per dataset/program across strata, models, and prediction/observation/control. Do not make per-stratum target removal the primary readout: changing the membership across perturbations changes the correlated quantity and can itself introduce between-condition variation.
4. Before current outcomes were inspected, the reviewer recommended a fixed existing benchmark feature mask for the primary readout if comparability to the benchmark is the objective, with all measured original members as a mandatory sensitivity. The stored `exclude_gene_idx` includes more than direct perturbation targets; it must therefore be named **benchmark feature mask**, not **direct-target exclusion**. A fixed union of directly perturbed genes across the evaluated task/unit is a separately named sensitivity when useful. Per-stratum direct-target removal can be an additional sensitivity, but is not needed to define the main result. Root's adopted choice should be recorded explicitly below; the important constraint is to freeze this choice rather than select a variant by correlation or number of finite results.
5. Separate input support from observed evaluability. Any shared-stratum restriction is defined by explicit unsupported/declined metadata on the fixed roster, not low correlation, constant predictions, weak effects, or large errors. The same label-based restriction applies to every compared model and floor. Report excluded labels, counts, and which model caused an exclusion. Preserve full-support results separately; the shared-support analysis is conditional on the retained subset.
6. Within that support, observed-only eligibility is at least three strata, at least one retained measured member, finite values, and observed score SD above the fixed numerical tolerance. Apply the resulting unit list to every model. Minimum three strata is a comparison policy, whereas a constant observed score is mathematical nonidentifiability. A nonzero SD is not evidence of biological reliability. Report member coverage and unit coverage so a one-gene score or two-dataset mean is not mistaken for complete program/task coverage.
7. A model with missing or constant predictions on a selected unit remains NA with its reason. Do not average that model over a more favorable subset; do not replace undefined correlation with zero. Missing/constant model entries must remain visible in source data and the figure's coverage annotation, so a finite best value does not hide unsuccessful candidates.
8. Use the fixed census roles: conditioned native/adapted predictors are candidates; FP-ridge, linear-shift-KOemb, universal floors, and other diagnostics remain separate comparators. Complete model execution and observed program evaluability are different requirements. Adding a model may alter shared support and require recomputing existing model/floor scores.

## Descriptive maximum and statistical interpretation

Panel b can retain the best-observed-conditioned lollipop, accompanied by all eligible model points. Compute each candidate's equal-weight mean of unit correlations on the same unit set, then take the maximum. Disclose the selected model, number of finite candidates, number of roster candidates, exact units, and aggregation. This maximum is selection-biased upward and depends on the roster; it is not an independently validated winner, a population upper bound, or evidence of improvement from adding models. Do not attach an ordinary single-model confidence interval to a selected maximum.

The recommended main figure has a zero reference and **no inferential gray null band**. The original plotting code sets the gray band from observed `best_corr` values classified as unsuccessful; this is not a valid null distribution and cannot be reused. “Recovered”, “weak”, and “at null” categories also require new evidence and must not be inherited.

If a permutation sensitivity is retained, its null and exchangeability assumptions must be explicit. Permute perturbation identity within the appropriate dataset/stratum blocks, using the same permutation for all candidates and programs and preserving repeated compound identity across OP3 lineages. Independent lineage permutations would discard shared compound dependence. Unequal compound support requires compatible support-pattern blocks or an explicitly separate common-compound sensitivity. Recompute all candidate summaries and the maximum in every permutation; selecting a winner once and permuting only that winner does not reproduce the selection. A max-selected null is generally not centered at zero, so do not draw a symmetric band by assumption. For formal claims across eight programs, additionally address those multiple comparisons. A permutation association result would still not establish superiority over the floors or biological validity.

Do not present a bootstrap over two eligible T3 dataset arms or four OP3 lineages as a well-supported generalization interval. Thousands of cells/compound strata do not supply independent dataset or lineage replication. Saved means alone do not reconstruct donor/cell uncertainty or refitting uncertainty. Per-unit values, coverage, fixed-metric sensitivities, and clearly labeled point estimates are the appropriate primary uncertainty display here. Existing intervals may be reused only when their exact metric, model, support, unit, and analysis are unchanged.

## Other panels

- **a: surface markers.** Keep the observed-versus-cell-mean marker comparison and its original role as a baseline-only protein readout. Large absolute normalized PD-1 effects do not establish a reliable biological receptor response; retain the assay/normalization caveat nearby. Weak PD-L1 signal does not establish a failure to recover a strong signal. Do not import RNA-model or RNA-to-protein conclusions.
- **c: program agreement versus response size.** Use an actual norm ratio computed from matched predicted/observed response deltas on one declared gene/stratum support. State whether the displayed value is a mean of stratum ratios, a ratio of aggregated norms, or another fixed aggregation; these are different quantities. Zero observed norm is undefined, not zero or an arbitrarily capped ratio. If x uses shared-support program scores, either compute y on the same support or explicitly label the two populations. A ratio of one means equal norm, not correct direction, calibration, or a baseline threshold. Do not retain a “success quadrant”, “magnitude floor”, or “higher is better” axis. Retain signed slope/alignment and full-support norm values in source data as needed to distinguish equal size from a reversed response.
- **d: lineage cases.** Retain scGen on Kang and FP-ridge on OP3 as fixed examples, not newly selected winners. Recompute each against the explicitly defined matched reference; do not substitute a different best model for the FP-ridge coordinates. Distinguish a floor fixed by task-average performance from a per-lineage maximum over floor members. State which definition is used, and avoid implying that an auxiliary subset equals the full census result. Positive point differences are descriptive; do not reuse stars or old seed intervals as significance claims. Newly improved conditioned models must remain visible in the other model-level outputs rather than being excluded to preserve the original story.
